# Report B: a game

Bar chosen: FREE-WITH-DATA is not used; PAYING bar, via ad revenue (a game earns from ads, not from users paying).

## Money, from pages I opened
- Wordle: sold to the NYT Feb 1 2022, "low seven figures", undisclosed (stuff.co.nz).
- Actorle (solo, Hungary): $4,000/month Oct 2022 (about $3,600 ads), 25,000 daily users at peak; about $3,000/month a year later, $50/month hosting. First Reddit post was lukewarm; growth came from a newsletter feature, shared results, then press (Indie Hackers; Indie Hustle).
- Plus15 (daily puzzle app): $18 all-time, listed for sale at $6,500, maker says it needs distribution (trustmrr, Oct 4 2026).
- Spread of outcomes: one sale in the millions, one steady $3,000 to $4,000 a month, one at $18. I found no source giving the share of daily-puzzle clones that get any players. Treat it as unknown, and the $18 case as more typical than Wordle. Heardle's Spotify sale appeared in search but I did not open it, so no figure is claimed.
- At $4,000/month, $1M a year would need about 20 Actorles. Ad income cannot reach $1M from one small game.

## Asks (demand first)
Weak. One dated item: Clue Funnel feedback (Dec 4 2025) that the share card is too subtle. I found no 2025 or 2026 review or post asking for a specific unserved kind of play. Demand is unproven.

## The candidate: Fewer Words
Each day there is one hidden target (a thing, place or scene, written by an AI). You write a clue; an AI guesser reads only your clue and tries to name the target. Score is how few words got it right. Alone on day one: you play against the AI guesser, try again with a shorter clue, and see your word count. Spread: the result link carries the target and your count and says "beat 7 words"; a friend writes their own clue, with no account needed.

## Scorecard
1. A daily game where you describe something in as few words as possible so an AI can name it, and challenge a friend with a link.
2. Exists? Not checked: no budget was left to search three wordings. Prompt-guessing and "describe it to an AI" games likely exist (Gandalf-style prompt games). Treat as probably not new.
3. Players are casual puzzle players; places to post: Reddit r/WebGames, Indie Hackers, Show HN.
4. One number: share of players who send a challenge link; test is 30 days of posting.
5. Odds: working version in two weeks 5 in 6 / 100 free users in 30 days 2 in 6 / 100 paying customers in 90 days not applicable (ads) / $1M a year in three years under 1 in 100 / someone bigger builds it in six months 2 in 6.

Searches for an existing version: none done. First-user answer: yes, plays alone vs the AI. The data: nothing valuable. Cost: each guess is an AI call. Grows by itself: challenge link. Campaign: no.

Verdict: weak. Ads-based games do not reach the bar.
