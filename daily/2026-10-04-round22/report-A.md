# Report A (round 22)

**Bar chosen: PAYING.**

**Idea (best of four, weak):** "Data Act Switch Page": a SaaS owner pastes their service name, export formats and notice period and gets the switching clause, exit-steps page and "no switching charges from 12 Jan 2027" statement the EU Data Act requires.

1. **What it is:** A one-click generator of the contract wording and public web page that EU Data Act rules require from any software-as-a-service provider selling to EU customers.
2. **Exists?** No generator found in one search (results were only law-firm notes: Mishcon, Latham, Pinsent, DLA). Closest: Termly-style legal generators (termly.io/pricing, free to 20 USD/month; no Data Act template mentioned) and the Commission's non-mandatory model clauses (per Latham; not opened).
3. **Who pays / where:** EU-facing SaaS founders. No place with a dated ask was found; zero ask records.
4. **Proof number:** 20 paid downloads at 49 USD from a landing page within 30 days. Cheap test: one static page plus form.
5. **Odds:** two-week working version 5 in 6 / 100 free users in 30 days 2 in 6 / 100 paying in 90 days 1 in 20 / $1M a year in three years under 3% (at 20 USD a month legal-generator prices, $1M needs about 4,200 paying customers, and no sourced count of EU SaaS vendors was found) / bigger builds it in six months 2 in 6 (legal generators add a template quickly).

**Searches for an existing version:** "EU Data Act SaaS switching clause generator template tool" gave only law-firm articles. Only one wording tried, so existence is not fully ruled out.
**First-user answer:** yes, output depends only on what they type.
**The data:** none; this is a paid chore, not a data product.
**How it grows by itself:** it does not. No audience; the hook is weak because the main duty has applied since 12 Sep 2025 and only the charge ban is dated 12 Jan 2027. Penalties are set by member states (Latham gives France 3% and Germany 4% of turnover as examples). No enforcement news found.
**Campaign:** no.

Not buildable without a gatekeeper.

- **EU Data Act** (above): weak.
