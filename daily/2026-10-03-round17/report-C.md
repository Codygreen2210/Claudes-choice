## Count
Six cases opened. True cold starts: 2 of 6 (Killed by Google, endoflife.date), with BundlePhobia launch failing. Layoffs.fyi, HIBP, SKG were not cold. A cold start is real but rare, and in both cases the topic was one every developer already cared about.

## Routes that worked for cold starts
1. Show HN with a plain title that states the whole list ("Site with EOL dates of everything"): 248 pts. The same format for Killed by Google ("part guillotine, part graveyard") got only 9 pts, so one post is a coin flip.
2. A stranger reposting the link to HN weeks or months later (Killed by Google: 102 and 62 pts). The list needs a name people want to share, and a household brand as subject (Google).
3. Reddit share by the maker (Killed by Google, per Techspot). Upvote count not found.
Pattern: first launch often fails (9 pts, 1 to 4 pts); survival comes from a memorable subject, a ready-made shareable title, and being re-submitted.

## "Features taken away after purchase" attention on HN
Named-brand, concrete stories do well: Motorola routers bricked 238 pts (2026-06); robot vacuum remote-killed 371 pts (2025-11); Jeep update 304 pts; Framework laptop 425. Brand-specific app complaints: Sonos app 3 to 81 pts. Generic phrases ("features removed after purchase") return only 2 to 7 point noise; "subscription required smart home shut down" returned 0 hits. So the subject draws crowds only through a single named incident, not as a category. Reddit numbers: one r/sonos post appeared via HN at 81 pts; I did not measure Reddit directly.

## Bottom line (honest odds, first-time unknown maker)
- 100 visitors in 30 days: about 60 to 70 percent if the maker posts once to HN and Reddit and shares it with 10 people; about 15 percent if no posting at all.
- 10,000 in six months: about 10 percent. The two cold starts here got there with a topic everyone knew (Google, end-of-life dates), not an obscure list.
- Single action that most raises odds: attach the list to one named, current, household-brand incident and post a Show HN with a plain title, then re-post it the next time that brand makes news (stranger reposts did the heavy work for Killed by Google). Press followed the HN and Reddit lift by about 6 months, not first.
Caveat: survivorship bias. These are the winners; I could not count the thousands of lists that got 1 point.
