# Lessons so far (read this instead of the whole idea journal)

One page. Add a line when a round teaches something new; do not let it grow past a page. Full detail: `daily/IDEA-JOURNAL.md`. Ideas already used: `daily/DISCUSSED.md`.

## Hard rules from Cody
- No idea that was already discussed, unless he approves it. Check `daily/DISCUSSED.md`.
- No planners or calendars. No gambling. Nothing from the tank car or railcar industry.
- Customer does one click at most: no installs, setup or choices before it works.
- Nothing that waits on a gatekeeper to respond or approve.
- Business ideas need sourced numbers, a customer base that verifiably exists, and a channel to reach it.
- Introduce ideas; do not build until he green-lights one.

## What sixteen rounds on Oct 3 showed (synthesis after round 16)
- **Reach is the wall, not the product.** Rounds 1 to 7: about 150 ideas, best odds 1 in 6 for 100 paying customers in 30 days. Rounds 8 to 16: 25 ideas checked one by one, none stood. Every idea that was new and worked for the first user still had no way to spread without an audience.
- **Every verified solo win had a head start:** a prior funded company, paid creators, a news hook, or one household name to ride. No verified cold start grew fast.
- **Most "new" ideas exist.** 9 of 25 were found live by the checker. Before claiming a gap, search three wordings plus: Hugging Face datasets and Spaces, the Apify store, the Wayback Machine, conductatlas.com, Product Hunt.
- **Odds by mechanism (checker's figures, 100 free users in 30 days):** users create the data, about 1 in 6. Page collects public data itself, 2 to 3 in 6. Press-cited public tracker, 40%. Nothing has beaten 10% for 10,000 users in six months.
- **Users creating the data fails three ways:** empty for the first user, nobody reports without a reward, and small piles are worth nothing (speech data about 160 euros an hour, sold in hundreds of hours).
- **Terms and law close doors.** Model output cannot be sold to train rivals. Voice is sensitive. Telegram bans building datasets from app users; Reddit's platform needs review and bars selling its data; TP-Link and Asus ban copying support pages. Read a source's terms before relying on it.
- **History that is "unarchived" usually is archived**, and history from public git can be rebuilt by anyone. What a maker owns is the checked, human-verified layer.
- **Hacker News shrugs at dry topics.** API breaking-change posts get 1 to 4 points; a named household brand gets hundreds.
- **Repairing twice does not help.** Three ideas got worse on the second repair. Retire after two weakened verdicts.
- **Agents grade their own ideas kindly; a separate checker that opens the links does not.** It caught false vendor numbers and links that did not say what was claimed.
- **Paying is far rarer than free.** Developer tools: free installs near 3 in 6, paying 5 to 10 people. Agents pay almost nothing per call yet (about $5k to $11k a month across x402).
- **Usage:** subagents are 82% of all units. Ten scouts a round cost 11.4M units a session; three to five agents cost 5.7M across nine rounds. A long chat adds up too (2.8M at 324k size). A chat caps at 200 web searches, about two five-agent rounds. Outside add-ons give about 2x to 3x, not 5x.
- **Small tests beat more ideas.** The checker pilot and the usage journal taught more than a round of proposals.
- **A proof step beats another opinion (round 17).** Twenty lookups to collect real events showed in one pass that most of "Pulled" was already covered by PIRG's graveyard, which three rounds of scorecards had missed.
- **Honest cold-start odds for a public tracker:** about 60% for 100 visitors in 30 days with active posting, about 8 to 10% for 10,000 in six months. A launch post can get 9 points and still be picked up months later by strangers.
- **The records themselves reveal the competitor (round 18).** Four proof files, 72 real records: 54 were already on a free list the inventor only found while collecting. A gap claimed by "vendors nobody covers" was false once the competitor's full vendor list was opened. Household-name angles are the most covered ground; look for history nobody archives, with primary sources.
- **The paying bar hits the same wall (rounds 22 and 23).** Seven ideas, no inventor found three dated asks from real people. Paying ideas land on shelves priced $6 to $25 a month with free versions beside them; at $15 a month, $1M a year needs about 5,600 customers. Price rises do not leave a gap: free replacements fill it in months. Marketplace listings bring buyers but cap low (best named Apify seller: over $2,000 a month).
- **Wider field, same wall (round 24).** Apps, games and finance gave four more dead ideas. Daily games from unknown makers: no 2025 or 2026 case of one growing was found; a live twin of the invented game had 0 upvotes. Agents cannot read Reddit or Stack Exchange, so "find dated asks first" needs another source (app store reviews, or asks Cody collects himself).
- **Course study and the six gates (round 25, `daily/PLAYBOOK.md`).** Requiring a paid, stuck leader did not find an opening: the bottom of every market checked already held three to six cheap or free newcomers, or the platform gives the job away (Stripe). The gate nobody passed is "name ten real buyers": web search cannot do it. The schools say pick the buyers first; that step needs a trade Cody already knows people in (not the day job).
- **A new rule's chore goes to whoever already serves the buyer (round 26).** Four fresh, primary-sourced changes; each was already handled by the insurance agent, the payroll software, the platform or an existing vendor. Before inventing on a rule change, ask first: who actually does this chore, and does the body receiving it check it for free?
- **Even $1,000 a month is about 6 to 8% on a store, cold (round 27).** Shopify: 51% of 22,546 apps have zero reviews, a new app gets 0 to 2 store installs a week. Apify: about 25 users per listing. Game portals: about 1.20 euros per 1,000 plays. Stores do not bring buyers to an unknown listing; the first ten customers have to be won by hand.
- **Users only, the honest number (round 28).** Best cold-start idea: likely about 300 users in 30 days, about 1 in 8 to pass 1,000. One Show HN post is a lottery ticket: the same solo maker got 2 points, 2 points, then 1,680. More posts in more places and a friend-challenge card move the odds; the idea matters less.
