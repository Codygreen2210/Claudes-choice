# Stanford / YC notes: picking a market one person can beat
Opened pages: 8 (7 of them useful). Not opened (blocked or empty): Thiel CS183 notes, YC Startup School library pages, CS183B transcripts, Helmer's book.

## Finding the idea
1. Notice, do not think up. Ideas brainstormed at a desk are "made-up" ideas and rarely work. Start from a problem you or someone near you actually has. Named mistakes: made-up ideas, solving a problem nobody has, and filtering by market size too early. http://paulgraham.com/startupideas.html
2. Aim at a small group with a deep need, not many people with mild interest. Same source.
3. Look for "schlep" work: tedious jobs stay unsolved because founders' minds screen them out. Ask "what do I wish someone else would solve for me?" http://paulgraham.com/schlep.html
4. Thiel notes (second-hand blog, thin): distribution gets overlooked; "great products do not sell themselves." Check distribution before product. https://www.dealerrefresh.com/?p=10791
   Last-mover and monopoly ideas from Thiel: not opened.

## Is the leader beatable
5. Counter-positioning test: the newcomer's model is better, AND the leader would hurt its own profitable business by copying it. Ask: what would the leader lose by copying me? If nothing, it is not a gap. Vanguard vs Fidelity: Fidelity only "dabbled" because passive funds ate its active-fund fees. https://www.sachinrekhi.com/7-powers-hamilton-helmer
6. Helmer's bar: bet only where, even if the leader plays its best game, it can be taken off the board. It protects against the leader only, not against other newcomers copying you. https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-hamilton-helmer/
7. Small ignored segments first, then expand (PG). Facebook began with Harvard only. http://paulgraham.com/ds.html

## First customers, no audience, no ad money
8. Go get them by hand; they will not come. Install or set the person up on the spot (the Collison method). http://paulgraham.com/ds.html
9. Numbers given: 100 users plus 10 more a week is about 14,000 after one year and 2 million after two. Weekly growth rate is the metric. Same source.
10. Big launches do not matter much; partnerships rarely help early. How happy the first users are matters. Wufoo hand-wrote thank-you notes. Same source.

## Competition
12. Competition is a good sign only if you have a thesis for what incumbents overlook, or a neglected segment. It validates demand and being late rarely matters. http://paulgraham.com/startupideas.html
13. PG: far more startups sink themselves (infighting, inertia, ignoring users) than get crushed. The real risk is unseen rivals. Visible competitors give false comfort. http://paulgraham.com/startuplessons.html

## Testing demand before building
14. Blank: 10 to 15 conversations a week; 100+ interviews total; write each belief as a testable hypothesis and record expected vs actual result weekly. https://steveblank.com/category/lean-launchpad/
15. Blank, 2026 update: with AI, a quick built "snippet" or untested product replaces the old MVP for discovery. So a cheap live test is fine; a long interview phase alone is not the point. Same source.
