## A. Is a leader open to being beaten from below? (check all)
1. Leader ignores the cheapest, least profitable customers because fighting for them does not pay. Newcomer starts there. Source: https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/disruptive-innovation/
2. Leader's own spreadsheet says "move up to higher margin". Both sides think they act sensibly; that is the opening (steel mini-mills began with low-grade rebar). Same source.
3. Product is simpler, cheaper, more accessible, not "better". Disruption is not a better technology. Same source.
4. Non-consumers exist: people priced or skilled out of the market entirely (new-market foothold). Same source; HBR https://hbr.org/2015/12/what-is-disruptive-innovation
5. Foothold is modest and overlooked, then moves up gradually. If your entry needs to win the leader's best customers on day one, it is not this pattern. HBR link above.

## B. When the newcomer loses
6. You offer a better version of what the leader's best customers already buy (sustaining). A motivated leader wins that fight. Christensen Institute link.
7. Label check: HBR says "disruptive" is applied too carelessly to any newcomer that shakes up a market (Uber is its example of a non-fit). Do not call an idea disruptive without a low-end or non-consumer foothold. HBR link.
8. Not opened: Christensen's "performance oversupply" claim (customers over-served, so they stop paying extra). Recalled, not opened.

## C. Is the market worth entering? (Porter five forces, plain words)
Source: https://www.isc.hbs.edu/strategy/business-strategy/Pages/the-five-forces.aspx
9. Buyers can push prices down or demand more for the same money.
10. Suppliers (for you: API vendors) can raise prices or set terms.
11. Substitutes meet the same need another way.
12. Rivals fight on price, or the cost of competing eats profit.
13. New entrants face no barrier: scale, brand, switching cost.
14. Nobody-makes-money signs (same page): many equal-sized rivals, low growth, high fixed costs, hard exits, strong buyers or suppliers, easy substitutes, low entry barriers. A solo AI-API product has low barriers by definition, so you need something else that rivals cannot copy fast.
15. Not opened: Porter's "What Is Strategy?" (both fetches 404). Recalled only: trade-offs and fit are what make a position hard to copy.

## D. Jobs to be done
Source: https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done
16. Describe the situation where the customer struggles, not the customer type. Circumstance matters more than demographics.
17. A job has social and emotional parts too. Condo sellers won by helping retirees with the move and downsizing, not by better condos.
18. Compete against non-consumption: a badly done or undone job, not rival features.

## F. Evidence on what predicts success
Source: Mollick, Dynamics of Crowdfunding (48,526 Kickstarter projects), https://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/bhagat/Dynamics-of-Crowdfunding.pdf
20. Overall success 48.1%. Founder with 10 Facebook friends about 9% success; with 1,000 friends about 40%. Audience size predicts outcome.
21. Video 4.3x odds; update within 3 days 2.73x; spelling errors about 13% worse; being featured by Kickstarter 20.47x. Being picked up by a channel beats product polish.
22. Caveat: this is crowdfunding, not software. The Penn Today piece (https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/everything-you-know-about-startups-wrong-says-wharton-professor) gave no numbers; only the claim that successful founders rarely look like the famous ones.
