# MIT notes (Disciplined Entrepreneurship, 15.390). Pages opened: 8 usable.
Several summaries are third-party reviews of Aulet's book, not MIT pages; marked (review).

1. Beachhead test, three conditions: customers buy similar products; they share a sales cycle and expect value the same way; word of mouth already runs between them. If any is missing, it is not a beachhead. https://executive.mit.edu/launching-a-successful-start-up-3-the-beachhead-market-MC7FUMDZ6IU5AIPP4WGIPN2PZJI4.html
2. Pick one segment from 4 to 10 candidates and drop the rest. Spreading across markets lowers odds. https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/disciplined-entrepreneurship-2nd/9781394222513/c02.xhtml
3. Beachhead extra filters (review): customer has budget, you can reach them directly, the pain is strong, you can deliver a whole product, the founder wants it. https://tianpan.co/blog/2025-06-14-disciplined-entrepreneurship
4. Size bottom-up: users times yearly revenue per user. Example: 50,000 users x $100 = $5M. Aulet's target for a beachhead is about $20M to $100M a year, a market you can dominate quickly. (review) https://tianpan.co/blog/2025-06-14-disciplined-entrepreneurship and https://www.ybrikman.com/blog/2022/08/29/disciplined-entrepreneurship/
   Note: count customers you actually found in primary research, then extend. Do not start from a big top-down number. https://shreyapoddar.substack.com/p/disciplined-entrepreneurship-a-summary
5. Reach check: name 10 real people or firms beyond your persona who show interest. If you cannot list them, the beachhead is wrong. Know who champions, who pays, who influences. https://shreyapoddar.substack.com/p/disciplined-entrepreneurship-a-summary and https://www.ybrikman.com/blog/2022/08/29/disciplined-entrepreneurship/
6. Position test: draw a two-axis chart using the persona's top two buying priorities. You need the top-right corner against every alternative, with a quantified edge (for example "80% faster"). https://www.ybrikman.com/blog/2022/08/29/disciplined-entrepreneurship/
7. Core: something competitors lack that you will protect and grow. Ask what that is for your idea; "AI APIs" alone fails since everyone rents them (my inference, not from a page). https://shreyapoddar.substack.com/p/disciplined-entrepreneurship-a-summary
8. Cost to win a customer (COCA) = total sales and marketing spend minus existing-base spend, divided by new customers. The true cost is an iceberg: one example grew from $6,250 to about $125,000 once a 5% conversion rate, sales cycle and overhead were counted. https://live.ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-390-new-enterprises-spring-2013/1a01df3cc1f970393cda3dccb9e20390_MIT15_390S13_lec13.pdf
9. Rule: customer lifetime value at least 3 times COCA; value is 5-year profit, not revenue, with realistic retention. Same sources as 8 and https://shreyapoddar.substack.com/p/disciplined-entrepreneurship-a-summary
10. Price rule (review): capture about 20% of the value you create. https://www.ybrikman.com/blog/2022/08/29/disciplined-entrepreneurship/
11. Write kill criteria before you test: the threshold at which you stop. Rank evidence: stated interest is weakest, behavior stronger. https://henty.com/blog/the-discipline-most-founders-skip
12. MIT 15.390 stresses the founder as the salesperson: first customers come from direct selling, not ads. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-390-new-enterprises-spring-2013/pages/syllabus/

## Against LESSONS.md
- Agrees: reach is the wall. The 10-named-customers test and the word-of-mouth condition are the course's version of it.
- Tension: the course picks a market BEFORE the product and sells directly to named people. LESSONS ideas started from the product and found no buyer list. Start from a reachable group of 10, not an invention.
- Tension: the $20M to $100M beachhead and 3x rule assume paid sales effort. A solo free-user path does not fit that math; $15 a month needs about 5,600 customers for $1M, matching LESSONS.
