# Playbook: picking a market one person can take (Oct 4, 2026)

Boiled down from open course material read on Oct 4: MIT (Aulet's Disciplined Entrepreneurship, 15.390), Stanford and Y Combinator (Helmer's 7 Powers, Paul Graham, Steve Blank), Harvard (Christensen, Porter), Wharton (Mollick). Full notes with links: `daily/study/`. About 22 pages were opened; several were second-hand summaries, and pricing pages, Thiel's notes and YC's library did not load. Treat this as a checklist, not a degree.

## What the schools say that our rounds were not doing
1. **Pick the buyers before the product.** Start from one small group that buys alike and talks to each other (MIT). Our rounds started from inventions and then looked for buyers.
2. **"It already exists" is not a kill by itself.** It proves people pay. It is a kill only if you cannot say why the leader is stuck (Graham, Helmer). Our rounds dropped 15+ ideas on existence alone.
3. **You do not overtake a leader head-on.** A better version for the leader's best customers loses (Christensen). The opening is at the bottom: customers the leader finds too small or too cheap to serve, or people who buy nothing today because it costs too much or takes too much skill.
4. **Reach is still the wall.** Mollick's 48,526 projects: 10 friends, about 9% success; 1,000 friends, about 40%; being featured by the platform, 20 times the odds. This matches `LESSONS.md`.

## The gates (an idea must pass all six, with a link for each)
1. **Leader and its money.** Name the leader, its price (page opened) and a sourced sign that it earns real money. No paid leader means no proven demand.
2. **Who the leader leaves out.** Name the group: its cheapest customers, or non-buyers priced or skilled out. Evidence: a price rise, a removed cheap plan, a minimum seat count, dated reviews from small customers.
3. **Why the leader cannot copy you.** What does it lose by matching you (per-seat revenue, billable hours, commissions, ad income, a sales team to feed)? If the answer is "nothing", there is no gap.
4. **Ten named buyers.** List 10 real firms or people in that group and how each is reached. If you cannot, the market is wrong.
5. **The sum from the bottom up.** Buyers counted from a source, times yearly price. State how many customers $1M a year takes and what share of the group that is.
6. **Each customer pays back.** What a customer is worth over its life should be at least 3 times what it costs to win one. With no ad money the cost is the maker's hours: say how many per customer.

## Also from the notes
- A market with no barrier to entry, easy substitutes and many equal rivals makes nobody money (Porter). A page built on rented AI has no barrier by itself; say what else is hard to copy.
- Write the stop rule before the test. What people do counts for more than what they say.
- First customers are won by hand, one at a time (Graham, MIT). 100 users plus 10 a week is about 14,000 in a year. This strains Cody's one-click rule only for the maker, not the customer.
- Honest limit: "easily overtake the market" is not what any of these sources promise. They promise a foothold at the bottom that can grow.
