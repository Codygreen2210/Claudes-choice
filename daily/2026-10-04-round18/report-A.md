# Report A: paywalled or removed features after purchase (proof)

1. **What it is:** a free public list of tech features put behind a fee or removed after purchase, each with date and source. Dead and cloud-shut-off devices link out to PIRG.
2. **Does it exist?** Yes, in part. GNU "Proprietary Subscriptions" (gnu.org/proprietary/proprietary-subscriptions.html, opened): dated 2014 to 2025, about 10 paywall entries, no source links. Consumer Action Taskforce wiki (wiki.rossmanngroup.com, opened): CC-licensed, documents subscription-locked hardware, holds Canon. PIRG graveyard (opened): dead devices, not paywalls. Closest differs only in being wiki or unsourced.
3. **Who uses it:** journalists, buyers checking before purchase; place to post: Hacker News, r/rightToRepair (not opened).
4. **Proof number:** 13 records collected; 10 already in GNU's list, 1 more confirmed in the wiki (Canon), 3 not in GNU (2 outright: Zero, Echo; Cloud Cam unchecked). 9 of 13 records rest on the GNU page alone (no primary source opened).
5. **Odds:** two-week version 85% / 100 free users in 30 days about 50% / 10,000 in 6 months 5% / someone bigger builds it 15% (it already half exists).

**Searches for an existing version:** 3 wordings (list of features removed/subscription after purchase; tracker free database; enshittification tracker). General search found no dedicated site; roundup search surfaced GNU; Canon search surfaced the Rossmann wiki. 16 of 16 searches/fetches used; search for Consumer Reports and iFixit not done.
**First-user answer:** yes, a lookup by brand works with zero crowd data, but only about 13 events.
**The data:** one record = brand, product, what changed, date, source. Ten thousand records would be valuable but the world produces few well-sourced events: the GNU list shows 2 in 2025 and 1 in 2024; my broader sample gives about 1 to 3 new notable events a quarter (honest estimate, not measured). No paying buyer found.
**Growth:** only through one named-brand incident. Attention seen: BMW heated seats (TechSpot, Motor1, TechRadar), Echo (AP via NY1, TechCrunch, 2025-03), Canon (Slashdot, 2025-01), Peloton (Techdirt, 2021). Each drew press as an event; none credited a list.
**Campaign:** weak; hook would be "what Canon, Echo and BMW took back", but a wiki and GNU already hold the same.
