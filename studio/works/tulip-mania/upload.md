February 1637, the Dutch Republic. In a back room in Alkmaar, a single tulip bulb, still in the ground, sells for more than 5,000 guilders, when a good craftsman earned about 300 a year. Two days earlier, in Haarlem, the buyers had stopped showing up.

This is the story of tulip mania: where the tulip came from, the "broken" tulips with flames in their petals (a virus nobody understood), the Semper Augustus nobody would sell, the wind trade in back-room taverns where people bought promises instead of flowers, the crash, and who ended up paying. And then the twist: how much of the famous story is true, and what the Dutch archives actually show.
